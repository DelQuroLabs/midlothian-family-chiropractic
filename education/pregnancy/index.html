Pregnancy & back comfort

Make space for questions about movement and comfort during pregnancy.

Call to schedule What to expect ↗

When to seek urgent care

Contact your prenatal team promptly about new or severe back pain, particularly with bleeding, fever, urinary pain, or possible labor symptoms. New loss of feeling in the legs or groin requires emergency care.

Keep your prenatal team involved

Back discomfort is common during pregnancy as the body changes. Your obstetric clinician or midwife remains the first point of contact for pregnancy concerns. Discuss new pain with that team before assuming it is a routine muscle or joint problem.

Share what matters

If you are considering a chiropractic consultation, let the office know that you are pregnant. Bring relevant medical advice and explain where you are in your pregnancy.

  • Describe your symptoms and how they affect daily activities.
  • Share any pregnancy complications or activity restrictions.
  • Ask about evidence, alternatives, and potential risks.

Make an informed choice

Research on spinal manipulation during pregnancy is limited. Any decision should consider your health, preferences, and prenatal care plan. Chiropractic care does not replace obstetric care or establish that a pregnancy is progressing normally.
